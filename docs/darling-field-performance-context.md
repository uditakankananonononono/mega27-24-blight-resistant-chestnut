# Darling identity and field-performance context: organizer claims, not a resistance benchmark

The American Chestnut Foundation's [performance page](https://tacf.org/darling-58-performance/) reports a Virginia Tech field trial with **5 of 24** oxalate-oxidase-positive progeny surviving to age 5, versus **19 of 24** negative full siblings. Arithmetic only: 20.8% versus 79.2%, a difference of -58.3 percentage points. The same page reports OxO-positive progeny **15%–25% shorter** on average than negative siblings at age 2–3 in Indiana. These are distinct sites and outcomes, not estimates of a single common effect. The [TACF discontinuation notice](https://tacf.org/tacf-discontinues-development-of-darling-58/) says the material formerly called Darling 58 was actually derived from a different prototype, and TACF withdrew its support for distribution petitions due to performance and identity concerns.

The organizer's live pages are archived verbatim with hashes and deterministic claim checks. The field counts are a single organizer-reported trial, not individual-tree measurements independently fetched by us. Site, family and selection context need care; neither a randomized intervention nor transfer to the Chinese-chestnut 2023 inoculation transcript cohort is established. In particular, one cannot cite these records as proof that the original Darling 58 line failed a controlled *same-task* benchmark or as evidence for any current deployment. The narrower verified finding is that a named lineage identity error and unfavorable reported field observations make historical generalized resistance claims unreliable. No individual accession payload, audited derivation, 50-page paper or project discovery/comparator gate credit is assigned.
