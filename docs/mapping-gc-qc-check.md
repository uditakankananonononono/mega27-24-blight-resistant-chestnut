# Chestnut 9h library mapping gap: GC co-variation, not a mechanism

The [original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9901152/) links a [15-library supplement](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9901152/supplementaryFiles) with clean/mapped reads, Q30 and GC percentage for each processed library. The earlier replay showed the 9h mock-versus-inoculated mapping gap. Here the same original DOCX is re-read, not a spreadsheet derived from the previous JSON, and its SHA-256 is pinned in `results/mapping_gc_coupling.json`.

For the three mock 9h and three inoculated T9h libraries, the *unweighted* mean mapped/clean percentage is 92.475% versus 87.127%, or -5.348 percentage points for T9h. Weighting by clean-read counts gives a close but different -5.335 points. Every T9h mapping percentage falls below every Mock9h percentage in these six rows. Mean reported GC is 43.833% versus 44.633%, +0.800 points in T9h; mean Q30 is 93.433% versus 93.330%, -0.103 points. Thus a second technical/sample-composition feature moves alongside the mapping gap, while Q30 moves little. This is a QC warning, not an explanation: with three versus three libraries, no per-read sequence or reference diagnostics, and no batch adjustment, one cannot assign the gap to GC, infection biology, cultivar, or reference bias. Any 9h expression comparison should first check mapping and composition sensitivity, and cannot call this source-table pattern resistance.

The replay script and test regenerate the contrasts from the archived original DOCX. This is one cohort's published quality table, not 15 individually fetched sequencing payloads, a comparator win, audited derivation, original discovery or paper-page gate. The direct FASTQ and independent phenotype validation remain open.
